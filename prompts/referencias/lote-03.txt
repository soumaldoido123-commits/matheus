Speech / Voice Acting / Lip Sync / Full Body Performance（very important）：


Dialogue must never be treated as audio only. Every spoken line must drive the entire body performance. Speech, facial acting, breathing, neck movement, torso movement, hands and weight shifts are synchronized as one continuous performance. Do not animate only the mouth and hands.

Lips stretch, compress, round and pull naturally depending on phonemes.


Mouth corners react emotionally, not just to pronunciation.


Cheeks compress and expand.


Small asymmetry is allowed.


Eye focus changes while speaking.


Breathing affects dialogue rhythm.


Tiny pauses, swallowed words, hesitation and interrupted speech are allowed.


Avoid robotic syllable timing.


Body reaction during dialogue:

Neck follows head movement with subtle delay and elastic return.


Shoulders rise during tension, surprise, hunger spikes and embarrassment, then recoil naturally.


Chest expands and compresses with breathing and emotional intensity.


Spine and hips shift body weight continuously.


Arms overlap naturally instead of moving together.


Wrists flick quickly at emotional moments.


Hands form readable poses.


Fingers have independent secondary motion and delayed settling.


Legs constantly rebalance body weight.


Feet adjust support and grounding.
